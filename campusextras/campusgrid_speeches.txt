SPEAKER 1 — Part 1 of 5
Slides: Title, The Problem
----------------------------------------------------------------

Good morning, everyone.

Think about the last time you had to find a lecture hall you'd
never been to before. Maybe you were late. Maybe you asked three
different people before someone actually knew the answer. Now
multiply that by every new student, every visitor, every semester,
across an entire campus.

That's the problem we're here to solve today. My name is [Speaker
1 Name], and together with my team, I'm here to present
CampusGrid — one grid, every path.

Let's start with what's actually broken.

Right now, campus life runs on scattered systems. If you're a
student here, you're juggling at least six different things just
to get through your week: a printed or PDF timetable, a group
chat for class updates, a noticeboard for event flyers, another
one for facility notices, a paper attendance sheet, and whatever
map you can find — usually outdated.

None of these systems talk to each other. Finding a lecture hall,
a lab, or an office still relies on word-of-mouth or guesswork.
Attendance is still taken manually, which eats into class time and
leaves records open to error. And information about what's
happening on campus right now — which hall is free, what event is
on, which route is accessible — lives in disconnected places that
nobody checks consistently.

There is no single platform that ties navigation, directory
information, and real-time campus data together. That gap is
exactly where CampusGrid comes in, and that's what my colleague is
going to walk you through next.

[Transition: "I'll hand it over to [Speaker 2 Name] to introduce
the solution."]

SPEAKER 2 — Part 2 of 5
Slides: The Solution, Six Pillars
----------------------------------------------------------------

Thank you, [Speaker 1 Name].

So if the problem is that campus information is scattered across
six disconnected systems, the solution has to be just as simple as
the problem is complicated: put it all in one place.

That's CampusGrid. A single platform that turns the campus into a
navigable, living map — where finding a room, checking your
attendance, and knowing what's happening right now all happen in
one app, in one motion.

Here's how it works in practice. A student opens the app. The Grid
locates them and guides them — whether that's to a classroom, a
lecturer's office, or an event hall. And as they move through
campus, the campus itself responds in real time — updating room
status, event schedules, and facility information as things
change.

To make that possible, we built CampusGrid around six pillars,
and I want to walk you through each one, because together they're
what make this platform different from anything else out there.

Pillar one is Navigation — turn-by-turn routing, indoor and
outdoor, between any two points on campus. No more guessing which
building the room is actually in.

Pillar two is Directory — a living index of rooms, staff, labs,
and services, searchable in seconds instead of asking around.

Pillar three is our AI Assistant. You don't search a database —
you just ask, in plain language, "where's my next class?" and you
get a route, not a link.

Pillar four is Accessibility. Every route CampusGrid suggests
accounts for mobility needs — ramps, lift access — by default, not
as an afterthought.

Pillar five is Real-Time Data — live facility status, room
availability, and event updates, exactly as they happen.

And pillar six is Sustainability — using route and usage data to
encourage walkability and reduce unnecessary energy use across
campus.

Six pillars, one grid. And one of those pillars — attendance
through real-time data — deserves its own spotlight, because it
solves one of the most tedious parts of campus life. I'll pass it
to [Speaker 3 Name] to show you exactly how.

[Transition: "Over to [Speaker 3 Name] for Smart Attendance."]

SPEAKER 3 — Part 3 of 5
Slide: Smart Attendance (RFID)
----------------------------------------------------------------

Thanks, [Speaker 2 Name].

I want to talk about something every student and every lecturer in
this room has lived through: attendance that takes itself.

Right now, attendance means someone standing at the front of a
class, calling out names, and losing five to ten minutes of
teaching time every single session. It's manual, it's slow, and
it's easy to get wrong.

CampusGrid replaces that entirely using RFID — radio-frequency
identification — and here's exactly how it works, in four steps.

Step one: Tap in. A student simply taps their existing campus ID
card on an RFID reader mounted at the classroom entrance. No new
card, no new device — just the ID they already carry.

Step two: Instant sync. The moment that tap happens, the tag ID,
the timestamp, and the exact location sync to the CampusGrid
backend in real time. There's no delay, no manual entry.

Step three: Auto-logged. That data is automatically matched
against the class schedule and recorded as attendance — no roll
call, no paper, no room for human error.

Step four: Visible to all. Lecturers and admin staff get a live
attendance dashboard they can check at any moment. Students, on
the other hand, can view their own attendance record on demand,
so there's never a dispute about whether they showed up.

This one feature alone gives back class time, removes manual
error, and gives everyone — students, lecturers, and
administrators — a single source of truth for attendance.

But attendance is just one piece of what makes CampusGrid
different from anything else on the market. I'll let [Speaker 4
Name] explain why no one else has put this all together.

[Transition: "Over to [Speaker 4 Name]."]

SPEAKER 4 — Part 4 of 5
Slides: Why CampusGrid, System Architecture
----------------------------------------------------------------

Thank you, [Speaker 3 Name].

At this point you might be thinking — navigation apps exist.
Directory apps exist. Attendance systems exist. So what makes
CampusGrid different?

The answer is simple: every other platform solves one piece. None
of them solve all six.

Generic map apps give you navigation, and that's where it stops —
no directory, no AI assistant, no accessibility awareness, no
real-time data, no sustainability insight. Campus directory apps
give you a searchable list of rooms and staff, but they don't help
you get there, and they don't know what's happening right now.
Attendance systems solve exactly one problem — attendance — and
nothing else.

CampusGrid is the only platform that brings navigation, directory,
AI assistance, accessibility, real-time data, and sustainability
together, under one grid. That's not a small feature difference —
that's a fundamentally different category of product.

Now, a platform this connected only works if the architecture
behind it is solid, so let me show you how it's actually built.

It comes down to three layers, working together.

The first layer is the client — the Mobile App. This is what
students and staff interact with directly: navigation, directory
search, and the AI assistant all live here.

The second layer is the backend — CampusGrid Cloud. This is where
the real intelligence lives: the routing engine that calculates
paths, the data synchronization that keeps everything current, and
the dashboards that lecturers and admins rely on.

And the third layer is the RFID and IoT network — the physical
sensors and readers spread across campus, feeding attendance data
and facility status back into the cloud in real time.

Client, cloud, sensors — simple on the surface, but fully connected
underneath. And that architecture is what makes it possible to
actually roll this out, starting small and scaling up. I'll hand
it to [Speaker 5 Name] to walk you through exactly how we get
there.

[Transition: "Over to [Speaker 5 Name] for the roadmap."]

SPEAKER 5 — Part 5 of 5
Slides: Rollout Roadmap, Closing
----------------------------------------------------------------

Thank you, [Speaker 4 Name].

We've shown you the problem, the solution, the six pillars, smart
attendance, and the architecture behind it all. Now let's talk
about how CampusGrid actually gets built — because we're not
proposing to boil the ocean on day one. We have a clear, three-
phase roadmap.

Phase one is the pilot, right here at KMKK. We deploy navigation,
directory, and RFID attendance in one residential college first,
as a live testbed. This lets us validate the system with real
students, real classrooms, and real feedback before we go further.

Phase two is expansion. Once the pilot proves itself at KMKK, we
extend CampusGrid to the neighbouring colleges — KMK, KMP, and
KMPP — all sharing one unified directory and one connected grid.
This is where the platform starts to show its real value: multiple
colleges, one system.

Phase three is scale. This is the full six-pillar rollout —
navigation, directory, AI assistant, accessibility, real-time data,
and sustainability analytics — deployed institution-wide.

From one college to a connected network. That's the path.

So let's bring it back to where we started. Right now, finding a
classroom, taking attendance, or knowing what's happening on campus
means juggling six different, disconnected systems. CampusGrid
replaces all of that with one grid, and one simple idea: every
path on campus, in one place.

We're not asking you to imagine a distant future. We're asking you
to picture a pilot — one college, one grid, starting now.

CampusGrid. One grid, every path. Thank you.
